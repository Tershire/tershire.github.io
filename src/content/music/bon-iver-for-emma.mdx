Justin Vernon retreated to his father's hunting cabin in northwestern Wisconsin in the winter of 2006, following a breakup and a serious illness. He recorded nine songs largely by himself, layering his falsetto over acoustic guitar and minimal production. He did not expect to release it.

*For Emma, Forever Ago* is the kind of record that makes you want to explain the circumstances of its creation, because those circumstances feel inseparable from the sound. The isolation is in the recording — the slight muddiness, the vocal layers that feel less like production choices and more like someone talking to themselves in an empty room.

*Skinny Love* became the record's signature song, though *Flume* deserves equal attention — its opening minute and a half is some of the most effective scene-setting in the last twenty years of folk music.

What's interesting is how little the context matters once you're inside the record. Knowing it was made alone in a cabin might draw you in, but it doesn't account for why it stays. The melodies do that. Vernon's ability to bend his voice into shapes that feel inhuman while remaining completely legible emotionally — that's the thing that keeps it.

A record about grief that somehow feels generative.
