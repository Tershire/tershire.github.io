OK Computer arrived in 1997 as something genuinely disorienting — a rock record that felt less like songs and more like transmissions from a society slowly losing its grip on itself.

Thom Yorke's paranoid lyrics about traffic jams, alien abductions, and corporate platitudes sat on top of Jonny Greenwood's guitar work, which ranged from delicate fingerpicking to walls of distortion that felt like machinery grinding to a halt. The result was simultaneously beautiful and deeply unsettling.

**What makes it endure** is that it aged into its subject matter. The anxieties it articulated in 1997 — surveillance, dehumanization, the creeping alienation of modern convenience — have only become more literal. Listening to "Fitter Happier" in 2024 doesn't feel like nostalgia. It feels like reading a headline.

*Paranoid Android* remains one of the most structurally ambitious singles ever released. *Exit Music (For a Film)* is devastatingly quiet. *The Tourist* closes everything with a plea to just slow down, delivered at the one moment in the record where the tension finally lifts.

A record that earns its reputation.
