There's something irreplaceable about the deliberateness of shooting on film. I shoot mostly 35mm — Kodak Portra for color, Ilford HP5 for black and white. The wait for developed rolls is part of the joy.

## Why Film in 2024?

I work with cameras professionally — depth cameras, event cameras, thermal cameras. I understand exactly how a CMOS sensor works, what noise characteristics look like, and why rolling shutter matters.

And yet, when I go outside to take pictures for myself, I reach for a camera that's older than I am.

Film is not a worse version of digital. It's a fundamentally different tool that produces a fundamentally different *process*.

**You have 36 frames.** That constraint forces you to think before you shoot. You look at a scene, you wait for the right moment, you consider exposure. Every frame costs something, so every frame gets attention.

**The feedback loop is broken — in a good way.** With digital, you shoot and immediately check the screen. With film, you shoot and wait days or weeks. This teaches you to trust your instincts rather than chasing optimization.

**The tonality is genuinely different.** Kodak Portra's skin tones, Fuji Velvia's saturation, Ilford HP5's grain — these have a character that's difficult to replicate in post-processing, despite what presets claim.

## Current Setup

- **Pentax K1000** — fully mechanical, 100% reliable, built in 1976. No batteries required. It's the camera I learned on.
- **Contax T2** — for when I want to carry something small. Expensive and fragile, but the Zeiss lens is worth every compromise.

## Developing at Home

About a year ago I started developing my own black and white film. It's surprisingly simple: mix chemicals, pour into a light-tight tank, agitate, rinse.

The first roll I developed myself felt like a small miracle. The images emerging on the strip — images I had composed and exposed weeks earlier — felt genuinely earned.

Color (C-41 process) is more temperature-sensitive, so I still send that out. But B&W at home is now a ritual I look forward to.
